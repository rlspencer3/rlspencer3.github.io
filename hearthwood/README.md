# Hearthwood

A cozy little house-building game for your phone or computer. Lay a stone foundation on a quiet meadow, raise the
walls board by board, swap in windows and a front door, stack the roof up to the ridge and light the chimney. Then
fill the rooms with quilted beds, bookshelves and a sleepy cat, plant an apple tree and watch the windows glow as the
sun goes down. There's no timer and no score, just you and your home.

Play it at **https://rlspencer3.github.io/hearthwood/** (or run it locally, see [For developers](#for-developers)).

## The screen

- **Top bar:** Menu, the piece counter, the floor control (▼ Floor 1 ▲) and Undo / Redo.
- **Rail (right):** the tools Build, Erase, Paint and Pick, then Look / Draw and All / Cutaway. It always stays below
  the top bar. When room is short (small phones, or the taller Paint tray) its buttons get a little shorter, then
  Look / Draw and All / Cutaway move beside the tools (not on landscape phones), and on very short screens the buttons
  show icons only.
- **Tray (bottom):** the options for the piece in hand (material, colour, accent, roof height, turn), the category tabs
  and the piece cards. The little handle above it hides or shows the pieces. On landscape phones the tabs fold into one
  category button beside the cards.

## Playing on a phone

| Gesture | What it does |
|---|---|
| Tap | Place the piece in hand on the glowing spot. With Erase, Paint or Pick, act on the piece you tap. A preview shows as soon as your finger lands. |
| Press and hold (about half a second) | Aim: the preview follows your finger. Slide to adjust, lift to place. Lift over the buttons or off the edge of the screen to cancel. |
| Drag one finger (Look) | Turn around whatever is in the middle of the screen. |
| Drag one finger (Draw) | Place a row of pieces. With Erase or Paint, tidy away or paint everything you drag across. |
| Pinch | Zoom in and out around the spot between your fingers. |
| Drag two fingers | Slide the view; the ground under your fingers stays under them. |
| Twist two fingers | Turn the view around the middle of the house under your fingers. |

- A second finger put down just after you start a Draw stroke turns it into a pinch and takes the stroke back.
- In Draw mode a held finger doesn't aim; it starts a stroke as soon as it moves.
- Long-press a piece card (or tap its ⓘ) to read about it: where it goes, how many materials, whether it can be
  painted, which floors it fits. **Use this piece** picks it up.
- Swipe the menu's header down to close it.
- Tap a villager (or the bubble over them) to open their card. With a piece in hand only a tap right on them
  does that, so you can build close beside them.
- Your phone gives a tiny buzz when a piece lands (where the browser allows it).
- A few gentle tips show up once each: tapping several walls one by one suggests Draw mode, and undoing a tap
  right away suggests press-and-hold.

## Tools

| Tool | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Build** | Places the piece in hand. A piece placed where another one is replaces it: tap a wall with a window and the window swaps in (windows and doors also swap walls on other floors you can see). The tray shows the piece's options: its material, its **Colour** (paintable materials only; *Natural* goes back to the plain look), an **Accent** colour for doors, frames and shutters, **Height** ▲▼ for roof pieces (*Auto*, or nudged up to 8 half-steps either way) and **Turn** (or **Flip** for pieces on grid lines). |
| **Erase** | Tap a piece to tidy it away. In Draw mode, drag across many. One stroke only clears one kind of thing on one floor, so wiping across a roof won't eat the walls underneath. |
| **Paint** | Pick a material (the Walls / Roof / Floors / Trim buttons jump to each group), a colour (24 to choose from), or both, then tap pieces. *Keep* leaves that part as it is. **Main / Accent** switches to painting door, frame and shutter colours. A material a piece can't use is skipped, and colour only shows on paintable materials. A material you pick stays where you tapped it, the strip scrolls just enough to show it whole, and its group's button lights up. On very short portrait screens the hint row hides and **Done** sits beside Main / Accent. |
| **Pick** | Tap a placed piece to copy it: the piece, its material, colours, accent and facing, and its floor (except for roof pieces). Then you're back to Build with it in hand. |

**Look and Draw.** In Look mode (✋) one-finger drags turn the view. In Draw mode (✏️) they place a row of pieces
instead; pinch, twist and two-finger drags still move the camera. A small *Draw* chip under the menu button reminds
you it is on (tap it to go back to Look). Wall-like pieces keep to their grid line while your finger drifts a little.
A turn makes a corner at the grid point nearest your finger, whether it is sharp or a lazy curve: overshoot it a
little and the extra bit is taken back, and the new side fills in from the corner. If a wobbly thumb puts the
corner one line off, the new side moves over to the right line once you settle onto it within a few cells. Drifting
off gently sideways moves the row onto the next line. Drawing over walls you've built gives them the new look, round
the corner too. Windows, doors, archways and gables in the way stay put, and the windows and doors take the row's
new wall look (their accent colour stays). To turn one back into a wall, tap it with a Wall. A whole stroke, like a
stamped blueprint, a loaded build or a new meadow, is one Undo step.

**Mending a gap.** Tap the middle of a missing wall segment, between two walls, and the wall goes right into the gap.

**The pieces counter** (🪵) counts everything you've placed.

## Floors

There are five floors: **Floor 1** (on the ground) up to **Floor 5**. The floor control at the top moves the build
grid up and down. Going up keeps your view. Going down shows a **cutaway**, with the floors above hidden so you can
see inside, unless you're holding a garden piece or a path. The rail's **All / Cutaway** button (V) switches between them.

- To build upstairs, lay **Floor** pieces on the next floor up over your walls, and put **Stairs**, **Spiral Stairs**
  or a **Loft Ladder** on the floor below. The floor above opens into a stairwell.
- Pieces know where they belong. Choosing one that can't go this high (a tree, a foundation, a pond) takes you down to
  Floor 1. Stairs on Floor 5 take you to Floor 4, since they climb to the floor above. Cozy things take you to a floor
  that has something to stand on and peek inside with the cutaway. Garden things go back to Floor 1 too, unless the
  floor you're on has a deck or flat roof to plant on; tap the grass past it and they land on the ground there.
  Roof and gable pieces show every floor.
- Upstairs, furniture and garden things need a floor, deck or flat roof under them, and fences, hedges and railings
  need one beside them (a railing can also stand on top of a wall or a beam): nothing hangs in the air. From upstairs,
  a fence or hedge tapped or drawn on the grass lands on the ground. A fence stroke stays on the floor where it
  starts: along a deck's edge upstairs, otherwise on the ground.
- The category tabs hand you something that fits the floor you're on. Upstairs, the Floors tab gives you floor boards
  rather than a foundation.
- If you place something on a floor the cutaway is hiding, every floor is shown again so you can see it.

## Roofs and gables

- **Roofs:** pick a roof piece and tap the top of your walls. Panels settle on the walls under your finger whatever
  floor is active (Floor 5's walls too) and stack up toward the ridge by themselves. Use **Turn** so the two sides
  face each other, and **Height** ▲▼ to nudge a panel. Tapping low on a wall face, far from its top, gets a gentle
  *"Tap the top of a wall to rest the roof on it"* instead of a panel in the wrong place.
- **Gables:** pick a **Gable Wall** or **Gable Window** and tap the open triangle at either end of the roof. It fits
  itself to the slope, gentle or steep. Over the top floor it takes the place of the wall below as one tall piece,
  in that wall's material and colour. A window or door there stays put: the gable won't cover it.
- **Chimney:** stand it in a room, or tap it onto the roof and it stands in that spot on the floor below. Its stack
  grows tall enough to poke out of the roof above it, even up by the ridge. It has a crackling hearth, and smoke curls
  from its pots.
- Hip corners, valley corners (for L-shaped houses), flat roofs, skylights and a pyramid cap round things out.

Wishing Well, Washing Line, Chickens and Scarecrow. Furniture and garden things stand at the right height by
themselves, whether on the grass, on a path, or on a deck, floor or flat roof.

## Building styles

Each style has a blueprint you can stamp down and take apart to see how it's made.

**A-frame cabin** (blueprint: *A-Frame Cabin*)
- An A-frame is nearly all roof. Lay the foundation, then place **Steep Roof** panels before any walls (they rise a
  whole storey per cell). With no walls under them to rest on, roof pieces sit right on the floor you're building, so
  the roof starts at the ground. Turn the two sides to face each other.
- Fill the ends with **Gable Wall** and **Gable Window** in **Glass Panels** for a glowing glass front. Use
  **A-Frame Glass** panels for light through the roof, and Cedar Shingle for the back.
- Add a sleeping loft (Floor pieces on Floor 2 over the back, plus a Loft Ladder), a Wood Stove, a stone Chimney and
  a Porch Deck with Railing among Pine and Birch trees, a Fire Pit and a Woodpile.

**Log cabin** (blueprint: *Log Cabin*)
- Walls in **Pine Logs** or **Dark Logs**: the logs stack with chinking, and the ends cross at the corners.
- Shuttered Windows, a Door, Oak Boards inside, Wood Shingles on a gentle roof, log gable ends and a stone Chimney.
- A covered porch: Porch Deck, log **Posts** and **Beams** (posts grow knee braces), Railing, and the roof running on
  over it. Finish with a Rocking Chair, a Woodpile, a Lamp Post and pines.

**Timber-frame cottage** (blueprint: *Timber-Frame Cottage*)
- **Plaster** walls are half-timbered: dark timber posts and braces are built in, and the plaster can be painted any
  soft colour (the blueprint uses cream with teal accents).
- Arched Windows, a Round Window, a deep **Thatch** roof, Gable Windows, a brick Chimney and a Flagstone foundation.
- A Hedge with a gap for the gate, a path, Flower Patches, Lavender, a Birdbath, a Beehive and an Apple Tree.

**Farmhouse** (blueprint: *Farmhouse*)
- Two storeys: Floor pieces over the whole ground floor, Stairs from Floor 1, and walls again on Floor 2.
- **Clapboard** painted white, Shuttered Windows with navy accents, a **Tin Roof** painted barn red, and a brick
  Chimney.
- A front porch of Porch Deck, Painted Trim posts, beams and railing, with a **Flat Roof** over it one floor up.
- A Picket Fence (leave a gap for the gate), a brick Garden Path, a Mailbox, Vegetable Beds and a Pumpkin Patch.

## Blueprints and guided building

Open **Menu → Blueprints** and pick a house.

| Blueprint | Pieces | Difficulty | Steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tiny Garden Shed | 25 | Cozy | Foundation · Walls · Roof · Gable ends · Garden & finishing touches |
| A-Frame Cabin | 103 | Handy | Foundation · Walls · Deck & loft beams · Sleeping loft & ladder · Roof & chimney · Gable ends · Cozy interior · Garden & finishing touches |
| Log Cabin | 105 | Handy | Foundation · Walls · Porch, posts & beams · Roof & chimney · Gable ends · Cozy interior · Garden & finishing touches |
| Timber-Frame Cottage | 104 | Handy | Foundation · Walls · Roof & chimney · Gable ends · Cozy interior · Garden & finishing touches |
| Farmhouse | 184 | Crafty | Foundation · Ground-floor walls · Porch, posts & beams · Upper floor & stairs · Upper walls · Roof & chimney · Gable ends · Cozy interior · Garden & finishing touches |

- **Place it instantly** stamps the whole house down. Pieces pop in step by step, and it's one Undo step.
- **Build it with guides** shows glowing ghosts of the pieces: bright for this step, faint for the next. The guide
  card at the top shows the step and your progress. **Next piece** hands you the next missing piece with the right
  material, colour, facing, floor and roof height, and turns the camera toward it. Taps snap to the glowing guides,
  and while a piece still has guides, taps elsewhere are politely refused. Any material counts, so make it your own
  (the blueprint's own material earns a little sparkle). Each finished step says so and names the next one. At the
  end the camera glides out to show the whole house and the card says *All done — welcome home!* **Stop** leaves
  the guides any time. On landscape phones the tray tucks away while you follow guides.
- If your meadow isn't empty you can **Clear & start** or **Keep my build**. On your first visit the meadow has a
  few trees, a bench, a path, a mailbox and a lamp. That scenery isn't your build, so it's cleared without asking.

## Villagers

Build a home and someone may move in. A **home** is a room with walls, a roof, a way in (a door, or just an open
doorway) and a bed; one gap in the walls or one hole in the roof is forgiven. A few seconds after your first home
qualifies, a traveller comes up the lane, knocks, and the windows light up: they live there now, one per bed (up to 4
a house, 20 in all).

* **Jobs.** Eight villagers: the Gardener (flowers round the house), Farmer (vegetable beds, hay, chickens),
  Woodcutter (a woodpile and trees), Fisher (a lily pond), Beekeeper (a hive among flowers), Baker (a kitchen inside
  and a bench or table by the door), Lamplighter (lamp posts) and Postie (a mailbox and a second home). What you put
  round a house decides who comes; the town card lists who could move in and what each would need. The very first
  home always gets someone, recipe or not.
* **Days.** Villagers wake, work, take breaks, visit each other, gather by the fire pit in the evening, go home and
  sleep; somebody is usually out with a lantern at night. Leave the day passing (the default) to watch it all, or pin
  the time in Settings and they keep busy within that hour. Peek inside with the cutaway: they really are in bed.
* **Wishes.** Now and then a villager would love something: a bench by the door, an apple tree, a fire to gather
  round. Tap the bubble, then **📍 Show me**: the camera turns to the spot, it glows, and the piece is in your hand, so
  one more tap places it exactly there. Wishes never expire with a penalty and never count down; one you ignore just
  fades with a smile.
* **Cozy.** The 🔥 hearth in the top bar glows brighter as the town gets cosier: granted wishes and new
  neighbours add warmth, and every different kind of decor round the homes adds charm. Tap it (or press T) for the
  town card.
* **Thank-you gifts.** Every few granted wishes a villager leaves you a gift piece (a flower cart, a hen house, a
  carved bear, a tea table…). It stays in your tray for good, villagers on or off.
* **Almost a home.** A building with walls, a roof and a way in but no bed gets a grey 🛏️ bubble for a minute; its
  card has **Show me** (a glowing bed spot) and **Just a shed 🙂**, which stops the hints for that building. Small
  sheds (6 cells or fewer) never get one, but anything with a bed in it does, however small. Walls straight on the
  grass work, and so does an open doorway instead of a door: one missing wall or roof panel is forgiven. Once you
  place what a hint asked for, its glowing spot goes away. A later home that no recipe fits shows a green 🏡✨ bubble
  with the closest jobs and what each would need (not while someone camping is about to take that bed).
* **Watching.** A toast with **👀 Watch** follows the traveller up the lane; tap a villager to see their name, job,
  trait and wish, and **👀 Follow** to keep the camera on them: pinch, twist and orbit keep following, a two-finger
  slide (or a pan) lets go. While a guided blueprint is unfinished, arrivals and new wishes wait (bubbles dim) and the first one
  comes when the house is done. Photo mode keeps the villagers in your picture and hides the bubbles.
* **Never lost.** Knock a roof off, clear the meadow or load another build: nobody is ever evicted. They wait in the
  broken house, move to a free bed, or camp in a tent by the lane until there's a bed again, and they remember the
  houses they lived in. Undo never takes a gift or a granted wish back.
* **A quiet meadow.** Menu → Settings → **Villagers & wishes** off gives the build-only game, exactly as before;
  nothing is lost and they come back when you switch it on.

One village lives on each device (it follows you between your saved builds and share codes). If you were already
building before villagers came, a one-time card asks first: **Lovely** or **Keep my meadow quiet**. The day now
passes by default so you can watch village life (a player who had pinned the time is switched once, with a note; pin
it again in Settings and it stays pinned). The full design is in [docs/LIVING.md](docs/LIVING.md).

## Saving, share codes and photos

- **Autosave:** your build saves itself a moment after every change, and again when you switch away or close the
  page, along with your view. It's waiting for you next time.
- **Save slots:** Menu → Save & Load has 6 slots, each with a name, a little picture, the date and the piece count.
  Saving over a slot asks first. Loading asks before replacing your build, and **Undo** brings your build back.
  Deleting a slot can't be undone.
- **New build:** Menu → New build clears the meadow (Undo brings it back) and hands you a foundation.
- **Share codes:** Menu → Share → **Create code** turns your build into a text code starting with `HW1:`. Copy it or
  use your phone's Share sheet. To open a friend's home, paste their code (it can be inside a chat message) and tap
  **Load this build**. It replaces your build as one Undo step, and the camera frames the new house. Codes carry the
  pieces only, not the name or the camera. A code with no home in it just gets a friendly note.
- **Photo mode:** Menu → Photo mode hides the buttons and shows the whole house, every floor with its roof on, even
  if you were looking at a cutaway. Frame your shot with the usual gestures and tap the shutter. When the cutaway
  would hide something, **✂️ Peek inside** at the top left (or V) shows the rooms for your photo, and **🏠 Whole
  house** puts the roof back. On a phone the Share sheet opens where the browser supports it; otherwise a PNG
  downloads. Tap ✕ or press Esc to leave: your floor and cutaway come back as they were.
- Saves live in this browser's storage on this device. Clearing the site's data removes them, so use a share code to
  move a home to another device. If storage is full or blocked, the game tells you once.

## Settings

Menu → Settings. These are remembered between visits.

| Setting | Choices |
|---|---|
| Time of day | A slider and Dawn / Morning / Noon / Sunset / Night (a preset pins the time). **Let the day pass** (on by default) runs a whole day in 10 minutes, with a long golden dusk. At night the windows, lamps and fires glow and the build grid dims. |
| Season | Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter. The leaves and grass change, and winter puts snow on the roofs. |
| Weather | Clear, Rain, Snow, Breezy (falling leaves) |
| Sound effects | Building sounds plus the meadow's ambience: wind, birds by day, crickets and owls at night, rain, a crackle near a fireplace, stove or fire pit, frogs by the pond at night |
| Music | A gentle generated kalimba and music-box tune over warm chords, slower and softer at night |
| Soft shadows | On / off |
| Villagers & wishes | On (the default) or off for a quiet build-only meadow; nothing is lost |
| Villager chatter | The villagers' little babble when you are near them, on or off |
| Quality | Low (screen resolution, less scenery), Auto (picks for your device), High (sharpest). Antialiasing changes apply the next time the game loads. |

All sound is made live in the browser, so there are no audio files. It starts after your first tap, as browsers
require. **Show welcome** brings back the welcome card, and **How to play** has the gestures, tips and keys.

## Install and play offline

Hearthwood can be installed like an app and then played offline:

- **iPhone / iPad (Safari):** Share → *Add to Home Screen*.
- **Android (Chrome):** menu → *Install app* or *Add to Home screen*.
- **Desktop Chrome / Edge:** the install button in the address bar.

It opens in its own window without the browser's bars, in either orientation. After your first visit (once it has
finished loading) every file is kept on the device, so the game starts with no connection at all. When you're online
it always loads the latest version and quietly refreshes its offline copy. Your saves stay in the browser either way.

## For developers

Plain ES modules and three.js r187 (vendored in `js/vendor/`, with its license). There is no build step and nothing
to install to play.

### Run it locally

Serve the `hearthwood` folder with any static server and open it in a browser with WebGL 2 and import maps (the page
can't run from `file://`):

```sh
cd hearthwood
npx serve            # or: python3 -m http.server 8000   → http://localhost:8000/
```

The service worker only registers on `https:`, so a local server never caches anything and your edits always show.

keys and layers, the piece record format, the `registerPiece` contract, the world / builder / camera / storage APIs,
events, the batching rules and the offline rules. To add a piece, register it in one of `js/pieces/*.js`. If you add
a new file the game loads, add it to `CORE` in `sw.js` and bump `VERSION`.

### Tools

Both tools serve the game themselves and drive headless Chromium through Playwright. Install Playwright where Node
can find it from `tools/` (for example `npm i -D playwright` in the repository root), then run
`npx playwright install chromium`. Rendering uses SwiftShader, so draw calls and triangle counts are real but frame
rates are not.

```sh
node tools/shot.mjs --query "bp=farmhouse&ui=0" --out /tmp/farm.png
node tools/shot.mjs --mobile --landscape --query "clean=1" --out /tmp/phone.png
node tools/shot.mjs --query "clean=1" --script my-test.js
```

`tools/shot.mjs` takes a screenshot and prints console errors, the piece count, draw calls and triangles. It exits 1
on page errors (console errors, uncaught exceptions, errors the game recorded); warnings are only listed.

| Option | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `--query "…"` | URL parameters (see below) |
| `--out file.png` | Screenshot path |
| `--w 1280 --h 800` | Viewport (the default) |
| `--mobile` | iPhone 13 emulation; add `--landscape` for 844 × 390 |
| `--wait ms` | Wait before the screenshot (default 1200) |
| `--eval "js"` | Evaluate an expression in the page and print the result |
| `--script file.js` | Run a file as the body of an async function in the page; `return` a value to print it |
| `--cam th,ph,r,tx,ty,tz` | Set the camera (theta, phi, radius, target) |
| `--timeout ms` | How long to wait for the game to be ready (default 45000) |
| `--allow-errors` | Exit 0 even with page errors |
| `--verbose` | Also print ordinary console messages |

In the page, `window.__hw` is the app object (world, builder, rig, blueprints, env, storage, ui, renderer…) and
`window.__hwReady` turns true once the game has booted. Dynamic imports inside a `--script` need absolute paths such
as `'/js/catalog.js'`.

`node tools/batch-regress.mjs` runs the 48-check static-batching regression in about 6 minutes: stamping, erasing,
painting, undo, floors, picking, touch, batched vs. unbatched pixels, and invariants in the middle of a blueprint
cascade. It exits 1 on any failure. Set `HW_ROOT=/path/to/hearthwood` to test another checkout. Run it after touching
`world.js`, `batcher.js`, `main.js`, `effects.js` or `blueprints.js`. `HW_TOWN=1 node tools/batch-regress.mjs` runs it
with the village's logic on (`town=fresh&townrender=0`).

`node tools/town-regress.mjs` runs the village regression (R1–R21 of docs/LIVING.md §13.4: homes, arrivals, Show me,
gifts, guided mode, photo mode, persistence, two tabs, never evicted, never stuck, the draw-call budget…, plus the
`repair1` to `repair8` sections' real-touch checks and repair5's overlap audit of full town days) in 10 to 30
minutes, depending on the machine, and exits 1 on any failure; `--only=showme,gifts` runs some sections, `--batch`
adds both batch-regress runs. The per-package checks run as `shot.mjs` scripts: `--script tools/town-brain.js` (the brain),
`tools/town-look.js` (the villagers' look) and `tools/town-hud.js` (bubbles and cards).

### URL parameters

| Parameter | Effect |
|---|---|
| `clean=1` | Start on an empty meadow |
| `nosave=1` | Don't autosave |
| `ui=0` | No UI |
| `bp=starter\|aframe\|logcabin\|cottage\|farmhouse` | Stamp a blueprint at boot |
| `showcase=base\|walls\|openings\|roof\|frame\|interior\|garden` | Every piece of a category in every material (rows = pieces, columns = materials) |
| `showcase=all` | Contact sheet: every piece once |
| `time=0.8` | Time of day, 0..1 (0.5 is noon) |
| `season=winter` | spring, summer, autumn or winter |
| `weather=rain` | clear, rain, snow or leaves |
| `level=1` | Active floor, 0..4 (Floor 1..Floor 5) |
| `seeAll=0\|1` | Cutaway / every floor |
| `cam=theta,phi,radius[,tx,ty,tz]` | Camera view |
| `welcome=1` | Show the welcome card |
| `thumbs=0` | No blueprint pictures |
| `batch=0` | Static batching off (for A/B checks) |
| `batchsize=n` | Shadow-region edge in cells, 1..20 (default 8) |
| `batchglass=1` | Also merge glass into batches |
| `town=0\|1\|fresh` | Villagers off / on for this load, or a fresh village in memory (the saved one is untouched) |
| `townseed=n`, `townspeed=n` | Seed of a fresh village; town clock ×n (1..120, the sky too while the day passes) |
| `spawn=gardener,baker` / `spawn=n` | Villagers moved straight into free beds (else they camp) |
| `wish=<template>`, `cozy=n`, `unlock=all\|ids` | The first villager's wish; Cozy ≈ n; gifts unlocked for this load |
| `townnav=1`, `townhud=1`, `townlog=1`, `townrender=0` | Nav overlay; dev HUD; log town events; run the village without drawing it |

Malformed values are ignored. The welcome card never shows on URLs with `showcase`, `bp`, `clean` or `cam`.
